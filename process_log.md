# Process Log — Task 06: Deep Fake

## Source Material

For this experiment, I used the coaching recommendation developed in Task 5 from my analysis of the 2015 Syracuse University women's lacrosse statistics.

The original narrative explained whether the team should focus on offense or defense, identified supporting player statistics, and recommended Kelly Cross as a player who could potentially develop into a game changer.

Because the original narrative was longer than the limits of some free generative AI tools, I created a shortened version that preserved the main recommendation and supporting findings.

---

## Approach 1 — ElevenLabs AI-Generated Audio

**Tool:** ElevenLabs
**Media Type:** AI-generated audio
**Input:** Shortened Task 5 narrative
**Output:** Synthetic voice narration

### Process

1. I initially attempted to use my complete Task 5 narrative as the input.
2. The ElevenLabs free version limited the amount of text that could be entered in a single generation.
3. I did not want to generate multiple audio files and combine them, so I shortened the narrative while preserving its main analytical findings.
4. I entered the shortened script into ElevenLabs.
5. I selected an available synthetic voice and generated the narration.
6. I listened to the generated audio and reviewed its pronunciation, pacing, clarity, and naturalness.
7. I saved the final artifact and clearly labeled it as AI-generated.

### Challenges

The main challenge was the character restriction in the free version of ElevenLabs. The original Task 5 narrative could not be generated as one continuous audio file. Shortening the script allowed me to stay within the free-tier restriction without having to combine multiple audio files.

---

## Approach 2 — HeyGen AI-Generated Video

**Tool:** HeyGen
**Media Type:** AI-generated avatar video
**Input:** Task 5-based narrative
**Output:** Synthetic talking-avatar video

### Process

1. I used the Task 5 narrative as the basis for the video script.
2. I selected a generic synthetic avatar rather than using or impersonating a real person.
3. I entered the script into HeyGen and generated a talking-avatar video.
4. I reviewed the generated video for speech quality, lip synchronization, facial movement, and overall realism.
5. I saved evidence of the generation process in the `screenshots` folder.
6. I clearly identified the resulting media as AI-generated.

### Challenges

The main challenge with HeyGen was the limitation of the free version. Although the platform allowed me to generate and preview the synthetic video, the free-tier workflow restricted direct downloading of the generated video.

This limitation was documented as part of the experiment because it affected how the generated artifact could be stored and reproduced.

---

## What I Learned

This experiment helped me understand the practical workflow involved in creating synthetic media using different generative AI tools.

With ElevenLabs, I learned how text length and free-tier restrictions can influence the generation process. I had to modify the original narrative while making sure that its primary analytical conclusion remained unchanged.

With HeyGen, I learned how an AI-generated avatar combines synthetic speech, facial movement, and lip synchronization to create a more visually realistic presentation. I also observed that adding a visual component introduces additional artifacts that may make synthetic content easier to identify.

More broadly, I learned that different generative AI platforms have different capabilities and limitations. Creating useful synthetic media requires decisions about script length, voice or avatar selection, output quality, platform restrictions, and responsible disclosure.

I also learned the importance of clearly labeling AI-generated media and maintaining evidence of the generation process so that the origin of synthetic content is transparent.
